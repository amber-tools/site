Give an agent your wiki without giving it a shredder

Atlassian's MCP server stops at Cloud. This one runs against the Confluence your company hosts itself, and hands an agent four operations with the guardrails built in: it cannot silently drop a macro, and it cannot publish over an edit somebody made while it was working.

Refusals reach the agent as plain text it can read and act on, not as errors it has to guess about. Works in Claude Desktop, Claude Code and any other MCP client.

No Markdown. No diffs. No agents.
Just the browser.

That is life on every self-hosted Confluence today. Atlassian's tooling only talks to Cloud, so nothing on your machine reaches your own wiki.

And the raw page format is a trap. One careless edit takes out the macros, the anchors and the column layouts, and a colleague finds out days later.

Three commands, and you are done

  • 1Pull. The page lands on your disk as plain Markdown with a small header.
  • 2Edit in your editor, your diff tool, your agent — whatever you already use.
  • 3Push. See exactly what will change first, then publish.
$ amber confluence pull 100000001
Onboarding  (v12, space DEMO)
~/amber/pages/100000001.md
3 opaque block(s): ⟦macro.toc#1⟧ ⟦macro.warning#1⟧ ⟦macro.status#1⟧

$ amber confluence diff 100000001
  ## Owners
- Analyst: John Doe
+ Analyst: John Doe, standing in for the release
+ ## Rollout
+ Staged, two regions first.

$ amber confluence push 100000001 -m "rollout section"
Onboarding  v12 -> v13

Byte for byte. Not best effort.

Every other converter reads your macros, turns them into something else and tries to rebuild them on the way back. That is where panels lose their colour, status lozenges turn into plain words and column layouts collapse.

This one rebuilds nothing. Whatever Markdown cannot carry is lifted out before the conversion and dropped back in afterwards, unchanged. A marker shows you where it sits, so you can move it or delete it on purpose — and cannot corrupt it by accident.

It will not let you overwrite a colleague

Publishing stops when the page moved on since your pull, and names who changed it and when. It stops again when a marker went missing from your text, and names every block that would have disappeared. Both exit with status 2, so a script can tell a refusal from a crash. 

$ amber confluence push 100000001
Refusing to publish: the page changed after you pulled it.
  your copy: v12
  on the server: v14, last edited by John Doe at 2026-09-20T09:14:00Z

Pull again and reapply your edit, or repeat with force to overwrite their work.
